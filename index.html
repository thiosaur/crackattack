Swap. Clear. Chain. Crush.

A fast-paced puzzle game. Match blocks, build chains, and bury your opponent under a pile of garbage before your own stack reaches the top.

  • Free and open source
  • Solo, against the computer, or against a friend
  • Easy to learn, hard to master
  • For Windows, macOS, and Linux

Inspired by Tetris Attack and Panel de Pon

Crack Attack! was originally inspired by Tetris Attack, Nintendo's puzzle game for the Super Nintendo — itself the Western release of Japan's Panel de Pon. The core idea is the same: swap blocks in a stack that never stops rising, chain your clears, and send garbage to your opponent. Daniel Nelson created Crack Attack! as free software; this version is a complete rewrite in Rust.

How to play

Seven short lessons. Choose a lesson or use the arrows to browse. Each animated demo loops through several examples. Use the Pause button to stop it.

1

The board and the cursor

The board is six blocks wide. Your cursor selects two cells side by side. Move it around the board to swap the blocks in those cells.

You can also swap a block with an empty space: it slides over, and then falls if there is nothing under it.

2

Match three or more

Line up three or more blocks of the same color, horizontally or vertically, to clear them.

  • five colors to match
  • New rows keep rising from the bottom. Clear blocks to keep the stack from reaching the top.

Clear four or more matching blocks at once, in one long line or connected lines, to send garbage to your opponent. The more blocks you clear, the more garbage you send. See the sizes.

3

Chains: let gravity work for you

When blocks vanish, everything above falls. If the falling blocks line up on their own, you get another clear — and a chain. A second clear makes it x2, a third x3, and so on.

  • In Solo, all the points of a chain are multiplied by its multiplier.
  • In a duel, a finished chain sends one big block of garbage to your opponent: the longer the chain, the taller it is.
Chains and combos. A chain is a series of successive clears, triggered when falling blocks form new matches. A combo clears four or more matching blocks at once. Some older Crack Attack! guides use “combo” for a chain.

4

Garbage: when you are attacked

Garbage arrives as a solid slab on top of your stack. You cannot swap it or clear it like ordinary blocks. Clear matching blocks directly next to it to make it break apart and release ordinary blocks.

The break spreads to other touching slabs of the same type. The released blocks can form new matches and help you recover.

5

Keep the stack below the top

The stack rises faster as the game goes on. The lights on both sides show its height. If it reaches the top, the rise stops and a seven-second countdown begins. Lower the stack before time runs out, or you lose.

  • Clearing blocks pauses the rise. The countdown also pauses during clearing and garbage-breaking animations.
  • Short of blocks? Hold Enter to raise the stack faster.

6

Gray blocks

Gray blocks appear occasionally. Match three or more in a line to clear them, just like colored blocks. Clearing them sends gray garbage across the full width of the board.

Gray garbage does not break when touching ordinary garbage breaks. Clear matching blocks directly next to it, or break another gray slab touching it.

How much garbage do you send?

In a duel, combos and chains send garbage to your opponent. Move the sliders to see how much. The sizes follow the game’s rules.

Combo size

Each slab is one row high. Clearing four matching blocks sends a slab three blocks wide. Clears of seven or more are split into multiple slabs.

Chain length

An xn chain sends one slab across the full width of the board, n − 1 rows high, up to a maximum of 11 rows. Longer chains send taller slabs.

Ways to play

Solo

Aim for the highest score you can before the stack reaches the top. Larger combos and longer chains earn more points, but your larger attacks return as garbage on your own board. Your best score is saved.

Versus the computer

Play against the computer at one of three difficulty levels: easy, medium, or hard. Practice your chains before taking on another player.

Versus a friend

Challenge someone on your local network, or connect directly by entering their network address. The last player still in the game wins the round.

Playing smarter

Clear blocks to buy time

The stack stops rising while blocks vanish. When you are in trouble, any clear buys you time.

Think in chains

Arrange blocks so that clearing one match makes others fall into place for the next. An x4 chain sends a slab three rows high across the full width of the board.

Break garbage to your advantage

Garbage turns into blocks when it breaks. Clear next to it and let the fresh blocks fall into place.

Keep the stack low

A low, even stack gives you time to think. Act before the countdown starts.

Raise the stack when you run out of moves

No lines in sight? Hold Enter to bring up new blocks instead of waiting.

Watch out for gray garbage

Breaking ordinary garbage will not break gray garbage beside it. Clear matching blocks directly next to the gray slab to break it.
