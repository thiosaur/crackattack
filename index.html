Swap. Clear. Chain. Crush.

A fast puzzle duel. Line up blocks, set off chains, and bury your opponent under a landslide of garbage — before your own stack reaches the top.

Inspired by Tetris Attack and Panel de Pon

Crack Attack! was originally inspired by Tetris Attack, Nintendo's puzzle game for the Super Nintendo — itself the Western release of Japan's Panel de Pon. The core idea is the same: swap blocks in a stack that never stops rising, chain your clears, and send garbage to your opponent. Daniel Nelson created Crack Attack! as free software; this version is a complete rewrite in Rust.

How to play

Seven short lessons. Pick one, or use the arrows. Each demo plays in a loop with several examples; pause it with the button.

1

The board and the cursor

You play on a board six blocks wide. Your cursor always covers two neighbouring blocks. Move it around, and swap the two blocks it covers.

You can also swap a block with an empty space: it slides over, and then falls if there is nothing under it.

2

Line up three — or more

Put three or more blocks of the same color in a row — across or down — and they clear.

  • five colors to match
  • The stack keeps rising from below, so clearing blocks is how you stay alive.

Clear four, five or more at once — a long line, or lines that cross — and you send garbage to your opponent. The bigger the clear, the bigger the garbage. See the sizes.

3

Chains: let gravity work for you

When blocks vanish, everything above falls. If the falling blocks line up on their own, you get another clear — and a chain. A second clear makes it x2, a third x3, and so on.

  • In Solo, all the points of a chain are multiplied by its multiplier.
  • In a duel, a finished chain sends one big block of garbage to your opponent: the longer the chain, the taller it is.
A word on words. In this game a chain is a series of clears that follow each other by themselves, and a combo is clearing several blocks at once. Older guides call a chain a “combo”.

4

Garbage: when you are attacked

Garbage is a big slab that drops onto your stack. You cannot clear it directly — but if you clear any block touching it, it shatters into ordinary blocks.

Garbage that touches other garbage shatters together with it. That is often your way back into the game, because the fresh blocks can fall into new lines.

5

Do not reach the top

The stack rises faster and faster. The lights on both sides show how high it is. When it reaches the top it stops, and you have seven seconds to bring it down — or you lose.

  • Clearing blocks pauses the rise, and the countdown does not run while blocks are vanishing.
  • Short of blocks? Hold Enter to raise the stack faster.

6

The rare gray block

Now and then a gray block shows up. It clears like the others, in lines of three or more, but it is special: clearing it sends full-width gray garbage.

Gray garbage does not break along with ordinary garbage. It only shatters when a clear touches it directly.

How much do you send?

In a duel, what you clear lands on your opponent as garbage. Try the sliders — the sizes are the game's own.

Clearing blocks at once

Each piece is one row high. A line of 4 sends 3 blocks of garbage; from 7 blocks up, the garbage is split in pieces.

Finishing a chain

A chain of xn sends one full-width block, n − 1 rows high (11 at most). Chains are the strongest attack in the game.

Ways to play

Solo

Rack up the highest score before the stack wins. Bigger clears and longer chains are worth more — but your own big attacks fall back on you, so it is a trade-off. Your best score is saved.

Versus the computer

A duel against the computer, with three difficulty levels: easy, medium and hard. The perfect place to practice chains before facing a person.

Versus a friend

Challenge someone on your local network, or connect directly by entering their address. Last stack standing wins the round.

Playing smarter

Clear to breathe

The stack stops rising while blocks vanish. When you are in trouble, any clear buys you time.

Think in chains

Set up blocks so that one clear drops others into a new line. A chain of x4 sends three full rows.

Shatter on purpose

Garbage turns into blocks when it breaks. Clear next to it and let the fresh blocks fall into place.

Keep it low

A low, flat stack leaves you time to think. Do not wait for the last seconds.

Raise when you are out of moves

No lines in sight? Hold Enter to bring up new blocks instead of waiting.

Mind the gray

Gray garbage ignores the shatter that wipes out ordinary garbage. Plan to touch it directly.
